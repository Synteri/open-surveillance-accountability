The Open Accountability Standard for Public Surveillance Systems (OASPS) is a proposed open public draft for research, discussion, and voluntary review. Version `0.3.0-draft.1` is currently a private bootstrap and has not been approved for public release.

## What this project is not

- **Not legal advice.** The repository does not create an attorney-client relationship and should not replace advice from a qualified lawyer about a specific situation.
- **Not an accredited standard.** OASPS has not been accredited, adopted, or approved by a standards body.
- **Not a certification program.** A case-study assessment is not a certification of a vendor, agency, product, deployment, or legal-compliance program.
- **Not a finding of wrongdoing.** The project does not claim that Flock Safety, Fairfield, any agency, any officer, or any other person has committed wrongdoing merely because evidence is incomplete or a proposed OASPS requirement is not met.
- **Not security-testing authorization.** Nothing here authorizes access to, testing of, interference with, or attempts to bypass any camera, network, vendor service, agency system, or account.

## Evidence limits

Findings reflect the public evidence available on their stated last-verified dates. Vendor documentation, agency policies, contracts, laws, configurations, and deployed behavior can change. A source may establish only what an organization stated or contracted to do, not what every production deployment actually did.

`Unknown` means that the public evidence reviewed was insufficient. It is a current evidence gap, not evidence of misconduct. Assessments against OASPS are judgments under this draft framework; they are separate from determinations of legal compliance.

Readers should inspect the cited sources, relevant dates, evidence labels, implementation states, unresolved questions, and responsible-actor assignments before relying on a finding. Corrections and contrary evidence are welcome through the repository's contribution process.
