# Connecticut ALPR Accountability Pilot

## Independent evidence for a decision Connecticut is already making

**The proposition:** Commission a public, vendor-neutral assessment of one real automated license plate reader deployment, beginning with Milford, Connecticut. The assessment will show what is documented, what is claimed, what can be independently verified, and what remains unknown.

## Why this matters now

<!-- oasps-citations:start -->

Milford is a timely first case. The city's current Real Time Information Center page links a Milford Police Flock LPR transparency portal and local ALPR policy. Connecticut Public reported a contentious August 3 Board of Aldermen meeting and prospective further consideration of a moratorium. Approved August minutes and the exact later action were not available from the official agenda center as of August 16, so this proposal does not claim that Milford enacted a moratorium. [SRC-0053, SRC-0055, SRC-0072]

Connecticut is also in a staged policy transition. Some Public Act 26-14 vendor-contract and public-record provisions took effect on passage, while major use, access, sharing, and retention restrictions take effect October 1, 2026. The act requires a new POST model policy by December 1, 2026 and agency implementation by January 1, 2027. The existing February POST policy remains a relevant current benchmark but is not represented as that future model. [SRC-0035, SRC-0037, SRC-0056, SRC-0074]

ACLU of Connecticut says it filed 94 public-records requests in November 2025 concerning state and municipal ALPR use and data sharing and lists ALPR guardrails and a municipal accountability toolkit among its 2026 priorities. That work shows an active statewide evidence need, but it does not imply ACLU endorsement of this proposal or any OASPS finding. [SRC-0059, SRC-0060]

## What already exists

The Open Accountability Standard for Public Surveillance Systems, or OASPS, is an open public working draft with 32 testable requirements, a source register, a claim-by-claim evidence method, machine-readable case-study ledgers, and publication-safety validation. It is not an accredited standard, certification, legal opinion, or finding of wrongdoing.

An independent standardized review is useful because a written policy, a vendor feature, a configured control, and proven operation are different things. OASPS keeps those layers separate and treats `Unknown` as a research result, not as proof of misconduct.

## Milford first-case deliverables

The **$5,000 Full Independent Assessment** will deliver:

- all 32 criteria applied to one defined Milford ALPR deployment;
- a criterion-by-criterion evidence ledger with source links and access dates;
- a Connecticut statute, POST policy, local policy, and contract crosswalk;
- vendor and agency claims compared with independently verifiable evidence;
- analysis of purpose, access, sharing, retention, deletion, auditability, misuse prevention, authorization, oversight, and remedy;
- unresolved questions and targeted, privacy-preserving public-records request drafts;
- prioritized recommendations for the actors able to implement them;
- machine-readable evidence and a plain-language public summary; and
- one factual-correction round without payer control over findings.

## Reproducible by design

Every consequential claim will resolve to a stable source record. Dates, evidence strength, implementation state, contradictions, and missing proof remain visible. Structured outputs and the public method allow another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning, challenge a citation, or update the case when new records appear.

## Known unknowns for the paid review

<!-- oasps-citations:start -->

The current public record reviewed for this brief does not yet establish Milford's complete executed ALPR contract and renewal history, current camera inventory, live sharing configuration, external-access records, completed quarterly audits, training completion, configured retention period, purge results, preservation exceptions, or end-to-end operating effectiveness. These are paid-review questions, not findings that the safeguards are absent. [SRC-0054, SRC-0070, SRC-0071, SRC-0076, SRC-0077]

<!-- oasps-citations:end -->

## Who benefits

Foundations and donors get a concrete public-interest output. Civil-liberties organizations get inspectable evidence. Journalists get a sourced and updateable record. Municipal officials get a neutral gap analysis and implementation roadmap. Technically sophisticated reviewers get machine-readable claims and reproducible provenance.

## Commissioning ask

Commission or underwrite the **$5,000 Milford Full Independent Assessment**: $2,500 at kickoff and $2,500 on delivery, with a target of 15 business days after kickoff and receipt of available baseline records.

For a broader founding commitment, a **$25,000 Connecticut Pilot** can extend the work to five to ten deployments and build comparative, recurring, open infrastructure.

To commission the work, reply to Jacob Schumacher at the address that delivered this brief. Public project information is available at [github.com/Synteri/open-surveillance-accountability](https://github.com/Synteri/open-surveillance-accountability).
