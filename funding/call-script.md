# Fifteen-minute funding call guide

## The outcome to pursue

End with one concrete result:

- funded engagement;
- decision meeting with the budget owner;
- fiscal-sponsorship next step;
- warm funder introduction; or
- meaningful institutional validation with a named follow-up action.

Do not end with only "keep me posted."

## 0:00 to 1:00: frame the call

"Thank you for making time. I want to use 15 minutes to learn what ALPR evidence matters to you, determine whether you are best positioned as a payer, validator, or introducer, and agree on one concrete next step."

### Thirty-second explanation

"I built an open, vendor-neutral framework for evaluating public surveillance systems. The first commercial use is a Connecticut ALPR Accountability Pilot, beginning with Milford. A paid assessment would compare policy, contracts, vendor claims, and public evidence criterion by criterion, preserve what remains unknown, and publish a plain-language summary plus machine-readable evidence. It is not anti-vendor advocacy, certification, or a legal opinion."

## 1:00 to 6:00: diagnose

Ask only the questions that fit the person:

1. "What ALPR decision, controversy, or evidence gap is most urgent for you right now?"
2. "Who currently has to answer that question, and what do they rely on?"
3. "Which evidence is hardest to obtain or trust: authorization, access, sharing, retention, audit, outcomes, or something else?"
4. "What would make an independent assessment credible enough to use?"
5. "Is there a decision date, meeting, grant cycle, or publication deadline?"
6. "Who owns the budget or approval for outside research like this?"

Listen for role:

- **Payer:** Controls or strongly influences a budget and has a live need.
- **Validator:** Has relevant expertise, evidence, or institutional credibility but does not own the budget.
- **Introducer:** Knows the payer or can create a warm path.

Say the classification aloud: "It sounds like the most useful role for you is [PAYER / VALIDATOR / INTRODUCER]. Is that fair?"

## 6:00 to 9:00: present the default engagement

"The default engagement is a **$5,000 Full Independent Assessment** of one deployment. It applies all 32 criteria, crosswalks Connecticut law and policy, compares claims with independently verifiable evidence, analyzes sharing, retention, auditability and abuse prevention, identifies unresolved questions, drafts targeted records requests, and delivers a public summary plus machine-readable evidence. Payment is $2,500 at kickoff and $2,500 on delivery. The target is 15 business days after kickoff and receipt of available baseline records. Findings remain independent of the payer's preferred outcome."

Then ask directly:

"Is this something [ORGANIZATION] can fund or commission?"

Stop talking and let them answer.

## 9:00 to 12:00: handle the response

### "We do not have budget"

"Understood. Is the issue no budget at all, the amount, the timing, or that this does not fit your mandate?"

If amount: "A $2,500 Rapid Review covers six to ten priority criteria, but the $5,000 assessment is the complete decision-ready product. Which scope fits the actual need?"

If no budget: "Who funds this kind of accountability work, and will you introduce me by email this week?"

### "Send me information"

"I will send the one-page brief, pricing, and Milford preview today. What exact question must those materials answer for you, and when should we schedule the decision follow-up?"

Set a date before ending the call.

### "We do not fund individuals or companies"

"That makes sense. I am pursuing fiscal sponsorship for grant-funded public-interest work while keeping separately commissioned work through the existing LLC. Would an eligible fiscal sponsor solve the barrier, and if so, what documentation or sponsor characteristics do you require?"

Ask who can confirm eligibility. Do not claim sponsorship is already approved.

### "We need a favorable finding"

"I cannot sell a predetermined conclusion. I can sell a rigorous answer, visible evidence, and recommendations that remain useful whether the deployment performs well, poorly, or cannot yet be verified."

### "Why Milford?"

"Milford is timely because it has a current deployment, a published local ALPR policy and transparency link, active public scrutiny, and new Connecticut requirements approaching. It is a useful first case, not a claim that Milford or Flock is uniquely at fault."

## 12:00 to 14:00: request the next action

Choose one ask:

- **Payer:** "Can we agree today to the $5,000 scope, subject to a short written agreement? Who must join the decision meeting?"
- **Validator:** "Will you review the five-criterion preview by [DATE] and tell me the single most important weakness?"
- **Introducer:** "Will you email-introduce me to [SPECIFIC PERSON] by [DATE] and say why this is relevant?"
- **Fiscal sponsor:** "What is the next required document or review step, and when can it be submitted?"
- **Government:** "Who owns the research, procurement, grant, or partnership route, and can we schedule a scoped follow-up with that person?"

If they offer a vague introduction, make it specific:

"Who exactly would you introduce me to? May I send you a three-sentence forwardable note today?"

## 14:00 to 15:00: close and read back

"Let me confirm the next step: [PERSON] will [ACTION] by [DATE], and I will [ACTION] by [DATE]. The decision we are moving toward is [COMMISSION / FUNDING / SPONSORSHIP / INTRODUCTION / VALIDATION]."

Send the promised material immediately after the call. Record the result and follow-up date in `targets.csv`.
