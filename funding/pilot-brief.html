The problem

Communities are asked to judge ALPR systems from policy language, vendor descriptions, partial records, and competing public claims. Those layers are not the same. A standard independent review makes the evidence and the gaps inspectable without starting from a pro-vendor or anti-vendor conclusion.

The existing framework

OASPS is an open public working draft with 32 testable requirements, a stable source register, claim-by-claim evidence labels, machine-readable ledgers, and publication-safety checks. It is not a certification, legal opinion, or finding of wrongdoing.

Exact Milford deliverables

  • All 32 criteria applied to one defined deployment
  • Criterion-level evidence with links and access dates
  • Statute, POST, local policy, and contract crosswalk
  • Vendor and agency claims tested against public proof
  • Access, sharing, retention, deletion, and audit analysis
  • Authorization, oversight, misuse prevention, and remedy
  • Unresolved questions and targeted records-request drafts
  • Recommendations, machine-readable evidence, and public summary

What remains unknown

The reviewed public record does not yet establish the complete executed contract and renewal history, current inventory, live sharing configuration, external-access records, completed audits, training completion, configured retention, purge results, preservation exceptions, or operating effectiveness. These are paid-review questions, not findings that safeguards are absent.4

Reproducible by design

Every consequential claim resolves to a source record. Dates, evidence strength, contradictions, implementation state, and missing proof stay visible. Another reviewer can reproduce the reasoning, challenge a citation, or update the case.

Who benefits

Funders: a concrete public output Civil liberties: inspectable evidence Journalists: a sourced updateable record Municipalities: a neutral gap analysis Technical reviewers: structured provenance Residents: a plain-language account

Scale path

A $25,000 founding pilot can extend the method to five to ten Connecticut deployments, compare results, add recurring updates, and build open public infrastructure beyond one vendor.
