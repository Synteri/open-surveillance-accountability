# Police Safe Corridor school-area cameras

**Inventory ID:** `CT-FAIRFIELD-SYS-006`

**Evidence label:** `Partially verifiable`

**Implementation state:** `Historical`

<!-- oasps-citations:start -->
## What it is

Fairfield's 2023 municipal records call this the Police Safe Corridor Program: police-monitored cameras around school areas intended to provide early threat indicators and rapid law-enforcement alerts. Late-2024 municipal records say initial portions had been installed and the program was seeking continuation funds. [SRC-0047, SRC-0048, SRC-0049]

## Operator / vendor

Fairfield Police Department is the documented operator. A late-2024 status packet links remaining program funds to a Flock Safety invoice, but the record does not establish Flock as the original 2023 vendor or vendor for every Safe Corridor component; the system and product therefore remain `Unknown`. [SRC-0047, SRC-0049]

CT Insider reported that the police chief consulted the school superintendent about Fairfield Police operation of cameras around school sites. No located source establishes Fairfield Public Schools as system operator, feed user, alert recipient, or camera-governance authority. [SRC-0050]

## Public purpose

Police records describe early threat indicators, target hardening, situational awareness around schools, and rapid alerts to patrol officers and school resource officers. These are stated program purposes, not independent proof of prevention or effectiveness. [SRC-0047, SRC-0049]

## What public evidence establishes

- A 2023 police memo described law-enforcement-only automated intelligence, real-time alerts, suspect-vehicle images, a $107,406 estimate rounded to a $108,000 request, and a written recurring-cost calculation covering ten camera licenses. [SRC-0047]
- June 2023 Board of Finance minutes record dispatch access, a proposal for 18 additional cameras, Bridgeport Fusion Center monitoring, and an 8–1 fiscal approval. [SRC-0048]
- A late-2024 municipal packet says initial portions were installed, records program expenditures, identifies a Flock Safety invoice for remaining funds, and includes a request to continue the program. [SRC-0049]
- Reporting attributed to the police chief described 12 then-installed off-site cameras, vehicle and physical-characteristic filtering, and rapid cellphone alerts. The 12-camera count is supported by reporting rather than independently by the located primary municipal records; CT Insider supplies the granular analytics. [SRC-0050]

## What public evidence does not establish

The record does not support a current total of 30 cameras: 18 were proposed, the written cost schedule covers ten recurring licenses, and the reported 12-camera figure is not an official as-built inventory. It also does not establish a current approved or deployed count, vendor and product schedule, accuracy, current analytics, or 2026 operation. [SRC-0047, SRC-0048, SRC-0049, SRC-0050]

The phrase about identifying categorical threats does not establish facial recognition or another biometric system. No public record located establishes every camera as AI-enabled. [SRC-0047, SRC-0050]

## Governance / authorization

Municipal records establish fiscal authorization and agency program descriptions, not a dedicated public surveillance-use policy. No system-specific public policy was located for acceptable searches, accuracy review, audit, complaints, or recurring necessity review. [SRC-0047, SRC-0048, SRC-0049]

## Data and retention

Reporting describes some vehicle and person-related search attributes, but no primary municipal record supplies a complete inventory of captured or derived fields. No program-specific source establishes retention, deletion, preservation, exports, secondary use, or audit coverage. [SRC-0047, SRC-0048, SRC-0049, SRC-0050]

## Access / sharing

The June 2023 Board minutes record Fairfield Police dispatch access and Bridgeport Fusion Center monitoring. The police proposal described alerts to patrol officers and school resource officers, but actual recipient applications, roles, and configuration remain unverified. Current users, permissions, school-district access, vendor access, external sharing, and audit visibility are unknown. [SRC-0047, SRC-0048]

## Current-status confidence

The program is `Historical` because primary sources establish authorization and at least partial installation through late 2024, while no located public record establishes its 2026 operation or configuration. The evidence label is `Partially verifiable` because counts and detailed capabilities rely partly on attributed reporting and conflicting planning quantities. [SRC-0047, SRC-0048, SRC-0049, SRC-0050]

## Relationship to OASPS

This page identifies the system and the evidence gaps without scoring it. The comparison with Fairfield's traffic-enforcement program is useful because systems within one town may have different public authorization and data-governance records; that observation is a research prompt, not a negative finding.

## Highest-value next evidence

A current program status, executed vendor and product schedule, aggregate as-built count, dedicated use policy, analytics description, retention rules, access roster, sharing arrangement, and aggregate audit information would resolve the principal gaps. Exact camera locations are unnecessary.
