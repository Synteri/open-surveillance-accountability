# Axon police video systems

**Inventory IDs:** `CT-FAIRFIELD-SYS-003` through `CT-FAIRFIELD-SYS-005`

**Evidence label:** `Partially verifiable`

**Implementation state:** `Deployed now`

<!-- oasps-citations:start -->
## What it is

Fairfield Police Department's current policy documents three related but distinct layers: officer-worn cameras, patrol-vehicle dashboard or in-vehicle cameras, and Axon digital-evidence and video services. The inventory keeps those layers in separate rows rather than calling them one camera system. [SRC-0043]

## Operator / vendor

Fairfield Police Department is the operator, and the current policy repeatedly identifies Axon services and features. Historical municipal records document the broader Axon acquisition, while the policy does not state current device counts or dashboard-camera model. [SRC-0043, SRC-0044, SRC-0045]

## Public purpose

The policy describes recording public law-enforcement interactions, preserving evidence, supporting investigations and authorized livestream assistance, responding to lawful disclosure duties, and conducting supervisory and administrative review. [SRC-0043]

## What public evidence establishes

- Body-worn cameras are required during defined public law-enforcement interactions, with activation, upload, supervisory review, access, preservation, and disclosure procedures. [SRC-0043]
- Dashboard cameras are required in patrol vehicles, with emergency-light or manual triggering, a 30-second pre-event buffer, uploads, and restricted investigative Video Recall procedures. [SRC-0043]
- The policy documents Evidence.com or Axon Evidence storage, docking or mobile-data-terminal uploads, Axon Respond Livestream, Axon Performance, Axon Signal features, role assignment, annual review of system audit reports, and randomized weekly supervisory review. The policy does not by itself establish that every named feature is licensed or enabled. [SRC-0043]
- A 2023 municipal status packet says equipment and licensing for the broader Axon purchase had been received except scheduled future upgrades; an earlier proposal named a Fleet 3 Advanced bundle. [SRC-0044, SRC-0045]

## What public evidence does not establish

The sources do not establish current body-camera or vehicle-camera counts, current hardware models, Fleet 3 upgrade status, complete feature and license inventory, configured role matrix, actual compliance, audit findings, vendor support access, integrations, storage architecture, or deletion results. The historical Fleet 3 proposal is not current-configuration proof. [SRC-0043, SRC-0044, SRC-0045, SRC-0046]

No Fairfield source reviewed separately establishes Axon ALPR licensing, activation, retention, hotlists, searching, or sharing. The current local camera policy contains no Fleet 3 or ALPR terminology, while Axon's generic documentation describes Fleet 3 ALPR as a separately subscribed and administrator-activated service; neither fact proves Fairfield uses it. [SRC-0043, SRC-0051]

## Governance / authorization

Fairfield Police policy §10.9 is the current operational authority located for body-worn and dashboard cameras. It assigns administrative roles, limits use and access, defines review and disclosure paths, and requires annual review of system audit reports and supervisory review; it does not establish an independent audit program, and policy text is not proof of incident-by-incident compliance. [SRC-0043]

## Data and retention

The policy sets at least 90 days for recordings that are not preserved and longer periods for evidentiary material, open cases, ongoing or anticipated proceedings, litigation holds, or longer state schedules. Other wording in the policy creates interpretive ambiguity for some longer periods, and no configured category-level retention matrix or deletion evidence was located. [SRC-0043]

## Access / sharing

The policy describes clearance-based Evidence.com access and defined roles for investigators, technical and professional-standards personnel, records and public-information functions, municipal and prosecutorial counsel, the Attorney General, retained claims representatives, and others authorized by the Chief. It also limits livestream access and Video Recall purposes, but actual accounts, exports, shares, and logs are not public. [SRC-0043]

## Current-status confidence

The current policy supports current operation of the body-worn and in-vehicle camera programs and their core evidence workflow, but public verification is partial because counts, models, configuration, enabled services, and actual control performance are not established. It does not prove that every named Axon feature is licensed or enabled, and the evidence does not support saying Axon replaced Flock. [SRC-0043, SRC-0044]

## Relationship to OASPS

These rows inventory devices and services without converting policy requirements into a full OASPS assessment. A later comparison should keep written rules, configured controls, actual use, and independent verification separate.

## Highest-value next evidence

The most useful additions are the current executed agreement and license schedule, device and vehicle inventory, enabled-service summary, role matrix, configured retention categories, vendor-access controls, and aggregate access, sharing, deletion, and audit reports.
