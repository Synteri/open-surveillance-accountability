# Automated traffic enforcement cameras

**Inventory ID:** `CT-FAIRFIELD-SYS-002`

**Evidence label:** `Verified`

**Implementation state:** `Deployed now`

<!-- oasps-citations:start -->
## What it is

Fairfield currently describes an automated school-zone speed-enforcement program operating continuously in six approved school-zone groupings. The current municipal page says warnings ran through May 2026 and citations began June 1, 2026. [SRC-0039, SRC-0040, SRC-0041]

## Operator / vendor

The executed agreement and current Town page identify Altumint as contractor. Altumint performs preliminary processing, while trained Town personnel make the final decision to authorize a citation. [SRC-0041, SRC-0042]

## Public purpose

The deployed Fairfield program is limited to covered school-zone speeding enforcement. Although the ordinance authorizes a broader ATESD category, the current Town page says school-zone speed cameras are the only ATESD type presently authorized in Fairfield. [SRC-0038, SRC-0041]

## What public evidence establishes

- Fairfield adopted a local ATESD ordinance, obtained CTDOT approval and a later amended plan, authorized and executed an Altumint agreement, and publicly reports an active program. [SRC-0038, SRC-0039, SRC-0040, SRC-0041, SRC-0042, SRC-0052]
- The ordinance requires trained Town review before citation, a warning period, citation notice and contest procedures, restricted personally identifiable information disclosure, limited storage, post-resolution destruction, FOIA treatment, and annual reporting. [SRC-0038]
- The Town page describes six active school-zone groupings and publishes 16 calibration-record identifiers; Altumint's original executed-contract baseline was 12 speed cameras. [SRC-0041, SRC-0042]

## What public evidence does not establish

The public sources do not reconcile the 12-camera contract baseline with 16 current calibration identifiers. They therefore do not establish a current physical-camera count, complete as-built configuration, actual deletion performance, access roster, or audit results. [SRC-0041, SRC-0042]

The Board minutes authorized an initial contract term not exceeding three years, while the executed agreement states a five-year initial term plus possible renewals. The registered record does not explain that documentary discrepancy, and this inventory draws no legal-validity conclusion from it. [SRC-0042, SRC-0052]

The ordinance and contract contemplate other traffic or companion technologies, but no current Fairfield red-light-camera deployment is established. Conditional Flock-related contract provisions and blank or contingent quantities do not establish that those products were ordered, installed, enabled, or used. [SRC-0038, SRC-0041, SRC-0042]

## Governance / authorization

Fairfield Code Chapter 102 Article II requires operation under a CTDOT-approved plan and assigns citation review to trained Town personnel. CTDOT's register lists Fairfield's plan as approved December 11, 2025 and amended May 1, 2026. The Board's June 2025 minutes record unanimous authorization to enter the Altumint agreement, subject to the unresolved term discrepancy described above. [SRC-0038, SRC-0039, SRC-0052]

## Data and retention

The ordinance allows storage only as necessary and requires destruction no later than 30 days after the fine is collected or the final hearing is resolved, whichever is later. The executed contract contains a different 90-day post-disposition period; the public sources do not explain how the provisions are reconciled in the configured workflow or verify deletion results. [SRC-0038, SRC-0042]

## Access / sharing

The ordinance restricts disclosure of personally identifiable information to covered charging, collection, enforcement, and adjudication functions, while preserving applicable public-record access without such information. The current Town page describes restricted authorized access, but no public account roster, access log, or independent control audit was located. [SRC-0038, SRC-0041]

## Current-status confidence

Current operation, vendor identity, school-zone scope, continuous operation, and Town citation review are `Verified` from primary public records. The physical device count and actual control performance remain bounded unknowns rather than reasons to downgrade the documented current program itself. [SRC-0041, SRC-0042]

## Relationship to OASPS

This page inventories rather than scores the program. The ordinance is analytically useful because one Fairfield system has unusually explicit authorization, review, appeal, disclosure, retention, destruction, FOIA, and reporting rules. That contrast identifies evidence questions for other systems without predetermining whether they meet OASPS.

## Highest-value next evidence

The highest-value additions are a current as-built schedule without sensitive site detail, a configured retention workflow, access-role summary, deletion/audit evidence, disclosure statistics, and the first annual report. No camera-location map is needed.
