# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

This project depends on good-faith disagreement. Participants may strongly criticize surveillance technologies, public policy, agency practices, vendor claims, or OASPS itself. They must do so with evidence, precision, and respect for the people involved.

Expected conduct includes:

- addressing claims, records, requirements, and system behavior rather than attacking people;
- acknowledging uncertainty and correcting mistakes openly;
- giving fair consideration to evidence that cuts against a preferred conclusion;
- distinguishing criticism of a system or institutional decision from criticism of individual officers, employees, residents, or contributors;
- making room for civil-liberties, public-safety, legal, technical, and local perspectives to disagree in good faith.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following behavior is not allowed:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, insults, or discriminatory conduct;
- doxxing or attempting to identify a pseudonymous contributor;
- publishing raw plate data, personal travel records, home addresses, private contact information, credentials, or other unnecessary personal data;
- targeting ordinary individuals, individual officers, vendor employees, public officials, or contributors for abuse;
- knowingly presenting speculation as fact, fabricating sources, or repeatedly making accusations after their evidentiary basis has been disproven;
- sharing exploit instructions, encouraging unauthorized system access, or using the project to coordinate interference with cameras or agency systems;
- disrupting discussion through spam, bad-faith repetition, or deliberate misrepresentation of another participant's position.

Publicly available information is not automatically appropriate to repost. Use only the minimum personal or operational detail necessary to support an accountability claim.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and other project-managed spaces. It also applies when someone officially represents the project elsewhere.

## Reporting and enforcement

Do not put sensitive personal information or vulnerability details in a public report. Use GitHub's private abuse-reporting features or a private channel identified by the repository owner. If no safe private channel is available, report only that a sensitive conduct issue needs review and omit the sensitive material.

Maintainers may edit or remove content, close or lock discussions, reject contributions, or temporarily or permanently restrict participation when necessary to protect people and keep the evidence process usable. Enforcement decisions should be proportionate, documented when safe, and should not be used to suppress good-faith criticism.

Anyone handling a report should protect the reporter's privacy, avoid unnecessary redistribution, and recuse themselves when they have a material conflict of interest.

## No endorsement inference

Participation, criticism, review, or contribution does not imply endorsement of OASPS, its author, or any finding. The project will describe a participant as an endorser only with that participant's explicit permission.
