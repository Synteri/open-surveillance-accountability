# Crosswalk: IACP and BJA ALPR Guidance

**Recognized sources:** IACP ALPR policy resources (`SRC-0005`) and the U.S. Bureau of Justice Assistance ALPR Privacy Impact Assessment / policy template materials (`SRC-0006`).

These sources are domain-specific professional and government guidance for law-enforcement ALPR use. They are not accreditation standards. They help test whether OASPS accounts for operational policy, training, access, dissemination, retention, auditing, privacy, civil rights, and civil liberties familiar to practitioners.

| Operational guidance area | Related OASPS requirements | OASPS surveillance-specific extension |
|---|---|---|
| Defined authorized use and written policy | OASPS-B02–B04, OASPS-F01 | Add a reviewable purpose to each search and require public authorization for the capability, not policy existence alone. |
| User authorization, training, and access | OASPS-B01, OASPS-B03 | Pair individual access and training with case linkage and narrowly flagged emergency exceptions. |
| Hot lists, alerts, and data quality | OASPS-A01–A04, OASPS-C01 | Publish how matching, confidence, derived attributes, and consequential inferences work, and retain attributable audit evidence. |
| Sharing and dissemination | OASPS-B05, OASPS-C02, OASPS-C06 | Treat sharing topology and changes as public, versioned, independently reviewable configuration. |
| Retention and purging | OASPS-D01–D04 | Distinguish ordinary retention, evidence preservation, exports, legal holds, backups, replicas, and derived indexes. |
| Audit and supervisory review | OASPS-C01–C06, OASPS-F02 | Extend beyond searches to the complete rights-relevant control plane and independent access to evidence. |
| Privacy, civil rights, and civil liberties | OASPS-B04, OASPS-C03, OASPS-F02–F05 | Require protected-activity restrictions, privacy-preserving records of blocked attempts, independent oversight, and remedy. |

## What this crosswalk tests

The professional guidance helps prevent a technically elegant standard from ignoring day-to-day agency realities. OASPS should remain feasible for legitimate investigations while demanding evidence proportionate to the power of the system.

OASPS's strongest additions—source/build/deployment identity, complete vendor-access logging, contract-to-policy consistency, and automatic restriction after material rights-relevant control failure—go beyond the operational policy scope of these resources and require separate validation.
